# Stakeholder RACI Template

## What this is for

A stakeholder RACI is a simple matrix showing who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed for each major decision or workstream in the integration. It exists to prevent the most common failure mode in integration governance: the moment when a decision needs to be made and nobody is sure whose decision it is.

RACI matrices are sometimes overused. For a simple integration, a stakeholder RACI may be heavier than the situation warrants. For complex integrations involving multiple workstreams, multiple stakeholders, and sensitive cross-functional decisions, a clear RACI prevents weeks of avoidable confusion.

## How to read RACI

**Responsible.** The people who do the work to deliver the outcome. There can be more than one Responsible per item.

**Accountable.** The single person ultimately answerable for the outcome being delivered correctly. There must be exactly one Accountable per item. If there's more than one, accountability is unclear and the framework isn't doing its job.

**Consulted.** People whose input must be sought before the decision is made or the work proceeds. They have right of input but not right of veto. There can be several Consulted per item.

**Informed.** People who need to know what's been decided after the fact, but whose input wasn't required. There can be many Informed per item.

The most common mistake is to make multiple people Accountable, which leaves accountability genuinely unclear. The second most common is to consult too widely, which slows decisions to a crawl.

## Suggested workstreams and stakeholders

### Workstreams

A typical IT integration involves these workstreams as RACI rows:

Integration governance and decision-making. Day-one readiness. Identity strategy and migration. Email and collaboration. Endpoint management. Network and infrastructure. Security and compliance. Application rationalisation. Vendor management and contracts. Support and service desk. Communications. Cost and savings tracking. Cultural and process integration.

Specific deals may add or remove workstreams. Don't include workstreams that aren't actually being managed; an empty row in a RACI is a sign that the framework is being treated as decoration rather than operating tool.

### Stakeholders

Common stakeholders to include as RACI columns:

Acquirer-side IT leadership. Acquired-side IT leadership. Acquirer-side CIO or equivalent. Integration management office or equivalent. HR leadership. Finance leadership. Legal and compliance. Procurement. Security and risk. Communications. Acquirer-side senior business sponsor. Acquired-side senior business sponsor. Major vendor account teams (where relevant for specific decisions).

Not every stakeholder needs to appear on every row. Filter for the people whose input or accountability genuinely matters for each workstream.

## A worked example

For the workstream "Identity strategy and migration," a typical RACI might look like:

**Responsible:** Acquirer-side IT lead, acquired-side IT lead.

**Accountable:** Acquirer-side CIO or equivalent.

**Consulted:** Security and risk, integration management office, acquirer-side senior business sponsor, HR leadership (because of the joiner-leaver implications).

**Informed:** Finance leadership, communications, acquired-side senior business sponsor.

The same RACI for "Vendor management and contracts" would shift significantly:

**Responsible:** Procurement, acquirer-side IT lead.

**Accountable:** Procurement leadership.

**Consulted:** Acquirer-side CIO, finance leadership, legal, acquired-side IT lead.

**Informed:** Integration management office, security and risk.

The exercise is less about getting the matrix exactly right on first attempt and more about surfacing the disagreements about who owns what. Those disagreements are easier to resolve before the integration starts than during it.

## Practical guidance

**Build the RACI early but expect to revise it.** First-draft RACIs almost always have at least one workstream where accountability is unclear. Working through the matrix surfaces the gaps.

**Get explicit agreement from the Accountable people.** A RACI that says someone is accountable, without that person knowing or agreeing, isn't operationally useful. Walk the matrix with the Accountable individuals and confirm.

**Keep it short.** A RACI that runs to five pages won't be referenced. One that fits on a single page or screen will be. If the matrix is getting long, the workstream definitions are probably too granular; collapse adjacent rows.

**Revisit at major milestones.** The RACI at announcement is rarely the right RACI at day 90. Review and update at each wave transition, or whenever a workstream's nature changes substantially.

**Use it when decisions stall.** The RACI's main practical utility is in moments where a decision isn't progressing. Pulling out the matrix and asking "who's Accountable for this, and who needs to be Consulted?" usually unblocks the decision quickly.
